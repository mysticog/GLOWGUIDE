Skin type one

Oily skin

Oily skin makes more sebum than it needs. That can mean a glossy finish, but sebum also helps keep skin supple.

Characteristics

  • Shine appears across the face within a few hours
  • Enlarged, visible pores
  • Makeup tends to slide or fade by midday

Common concerns

  • Blackheads, whiteheads and breakouts
  • A heavy feeling after rich creams
  • Dehydration hiding under the oil

Skincare tips

  • Use a gentle gel or foam cleanser twice a day, not a harsh one
  • Still moisturise, with a light gel or lotion
  • Look at niacinamide and salicylic acid
Skin type two

Dry skin

Dry skin makes less oil, so it struggles to hold on to moisture and can feel uncomfortable.

Characteristics

  • A tight feeling after cleansing
  • A dull or rough surface
  • Pores that are small and barely visible

Common concerns

  • Flaking and rough patches
  • Fine lines that look more obvious
  • Redness or itching in cold, dry weather

Skincare tips

  • Choose a creamy, non-foaming cleanser
  • Layer a hydrating serum under a richer moisturiser
  • Look at hyaluronic acid, ceramides and squalane
Skin type three

Combination skin

Combination skin behaves like two skin types at once, usually oilier through the forehead, nose and chin (the T-zone).

Characteristics

  • Shine on the T-zone, comfortable or dry cheeks
  • Larger pores around the nose
  • Needs that shift with the seasons

Common concerns

  • Congestion in the T-zone
  • Dry patches on the cheeks
  • Products that suit one area but not the other

Skincare tips

  • Use a balanced, gentle cleanser
  • Pick lightweight hydration for the whole face
  • Apply treatments only where they are needed
Skin type four

Normal skin

Normal skin is well balanced: neither too oily nor too dry, and it rarely reacts.

Characteristics

  • Comfortable through the day
  • Small, barely visible pores
  • An even tone and smooth texture

Common concerns

  • Occasional dryness or breakouts with stress or weather
  • Early signs of sun damage and ageing
  • Over-complicating a routine that already works

Skincare tips

  • Keep the routine simple and consistent
  • Never skip sunscreen
  • Add antioxidants and, over time, retinol
The foundation

Understanding your skin barrier

The outer layer of your skin works like a wall: skin cells are the bricks and lipids such as ceramides are the mortar. It keeps water in and irritants out.

When the barrier is damaged by over-exfoliating, harsh cleansers, sun or dry air, skin can feel tight, stingy, red or suddenly sensitive. Protecting it is the quiet secret behind most healthy-looking skin.

Signs your barrier needs care

  • Products that never stung before now do
  • Tightness, flaking or redness
  • More breakouts alongside dryness

How to support it

  • Cleanse gently and avoid hot water
  • Pause strong actives while skin recovers
  • Use ceramides, squalane and a plain moisturiser

Worth avoiding

Common skincare mistakes

Over-exfoliating

Acids and scrubs every day wear down the barrier. Start with once or twice a week.

Skipping sunscreen

UV is behind most visible skin ageing. Sunscreen belongs in every morning routine.

Starting many products at once

If skin reacts, you cannot tell which product caused it. Add one new thing at a time.

Skipping moisturiser on oily skin

Oily skin still needs water. A light gel keeps it balanced rather than stripped.

Chasing quick results

Most ingredients need four to twelve weeks to show a difference. Give them time.

Using hot water

Hot water strips natural oils. Lukewarm is kinder for every skin type.

Keep it simple

Daily skincare essentials

Four things do most of the work.

Cleanse

Remove oil, dirt and sunscreen without stripping.

Moisturise

Hold in water and support the barrier.

Protect

Broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher, every morning.

Be consistent

A simple routine done daily beats a perfect one done rarely.
